## [P1] Constructing a Regional Slang Lexicon for Electricity Demands via Soft-Label Bidirectional Anchoring and Active Learning

Rong Chang, Lijing Shi, Zhaoxiang Zang · [arXiv 2611.04231v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04231v1) · relevance 0.22

**TL;DR:** A weak‑supervision pipeline builds a high‑precision Yuxi‑dialect slang lexicon for power‑grid customer service, achieving perfect dialect precision but yielding a modest‑sized lexicon and limited generalizability.

**Problem**  
Power‑grid customer service increasingly relies on community social media where users describe outages and faults in informal regional dialect, producing non‑standard morphology and out‑of‑vocabulary expressions that make manual lexicon construction costly and degrade conventional NLP tools[1].  

**Method**  
The authors propose a weak‑supervision framework that (1) extracts bigram candidates via Pointwise Mutual Information anchored on 16 Yuxi dialect markers with bidirectional soft‑label power‑domain layering, (2) filters candidates with a four‑condition fragment filter, (3) scores retained candidates using a dual‑channel classifier that combines Sentence‑BERT semantic similarity to 65 power‑domain seed terms with a jieba‑based wordhood measure, and (4) applies uncertainty‑aware active learning with three query strategies (Margin, TD‑CAL, Random) and two oracle types (rule‑based morphological matching and a retrieval‑augmented LLM)[2][4]. Evaluation uses a higher‑capability LLM calibrated against human expert annotations via Cohen’s κ[2][3].  

**Key results**  
- The evaluator‑estimated dialect precision (DP) is 1.0 with zero false positives[1][5].  
- Soft‑label layering expands the candidate pool by 751 % (from 41 to 349 terms) while preserving the same DP[1][7].  
- Human calibration on 46 annotations yields κ_dialect = 1.0 and κ_power = 0.85[1][2].  
- The final lexicon contains 25–33 terms (4–5 of which are power‑relevant) across configurations[6][7].  
- Candidate generation extracted 349 candidates (347 bigrams, 2 unigrams); the fragment filter removed 291 (83.4 %) leaving 58 for classification[5].  
- The SBERT+wordhood classifier accepted 19 candidates (score ≥ 0.78) and placed 39 in an uncertain pool (0.45 ≤ score < 0.78)[5].  
- All six configurations achieve DP = 1.0[5].  
- Under the high‑acceptance LLM oracle (~89 % acceptance) all configurations converge to 4 power‑dialect terms, whereas the rule‑based oracle shows modest differentiation[2].  
- Ablation shows soft‑label layering expands the final lexicon by 213 % relative to strict power‑gating[7].  

**Limitations**  
The study is confined to a single dialect (Yuxi), domain (power grid), and platform (WeChat), with no empirical transfer tests[6]. It reports only precision‑oriented metrics; recall against a gold lexicon and full human evaluation beyond the 46‑item calibration set are absent[6]. The 11,177‑message corpus may not capture the full diversity of dialect expressions[6]. No comparisons with established phrase‑mining baselines are provided, and the active‑learning experiment is underpowered (only 39 uncertain candidates and a single random seed)[6]. The evaluator shares the same model family as the selection oracle, and cross‑model validation has not been executed[6]. Downstream task impact (e.g., complaint classification) is not assessed, and many pipeline hyperparameters were tuned without a held‑out validation set, leaving their sensitivity unanalyzed[6].
